To make this concrete rather than hypothetical, we built a full Addressable Market Map for Geron's RYTELO (imetelstat) in Lower-Risk Myelodysplastic Syndromes across Germany, France, Italy, and Spain.

Every figure in what follows comes from sources anyone in this room could pull themselves — EMA and AMNOG filings, published epidemiology, Geron's own investor materials, general medical literature. We built it with zero access to any company's internal data, Geron's included.

So it's illustrative, not Geron's actual forecast — but it was built exactly the way we'd build yours, and it should hold up to the same scrutiny your own team would put it through.
Walking the tool — Problem Statement ~3 min

Before a single number gets modeled, the tool demands the discipline you'd demand from a good analyst.

TAM → Problem Statement
Confirmed
Problem Statement
What exactly are we sizing, for whom, and why it matters now.
Needs-based market
Disease-modifying, durable anemia relief for LR-MDS patients who remain transfusion-dependent despite standard of care — not symptom management.
Core business challenge
Size the realistic patient and HCP universe RYTELO can access across Germany, France, Italy and Spain — informing launch sequencing and resource allocation, with Germany as the primary detailed market.
Boundary uncertainty
Diagnosis rates, transfusion-dependency thresholds, and prior-treatment eligibility vary enough across EU4 systems that the boundary has to be set explicitly before any number is credible.
Prescriber & center behavior
The HCP universe spans hematologists, transfusion-medicine physicians, hematopathologists, and referring GPs — segmentation has to go beyond specialty or geography.
Competitive & access pressure
Standard of care, emerging pipeline therapies, and country-level reimbursement all compress the addressable opportunity within the approved indication.

Then it commits to hypotheses specific enough to be wrong.

Not a research plan in the abstract — testable claims that each direct a specific piece of research, confirmed by a human before the model builds on them.

TAM → Hypotheses
Confirmed
H1

Addressable pool size

Materially smaller than headline LR-MDS incidence suggests: transfusion dependence, no isolated del(5q), ESA failure, and RS-negative status compound to leave a fraction of diagnosed patients eligible.

H2

German AMNOG timeline

Germany is first and largest, but the ~12-month AMNOG benefit assessment sets the effective reimbursed price — the German number is contingent on the G-BA outcome, not patient volume alone.

H3

Luspatercept sequencing

The real competitive constraint isn't ESAs, it's luspatercept — RS-negative and post-luspatercept patients are the highest-confidence initial cohort.

H4

Diagnosis & referral leakage

A meaningful share of eligible patients never reach a hematologist in time — GPs chase GI causes for anemia in the elderly instead of a bone-marrow-biopsy referral.

Walking the tool — Boundary, Dimensions, Segments ~6 min

Draw the boundary in writing, before anyone models a single patient.

AMM → Boundary
Confirmed
Market Boundary
What falls inside the market being sized — and what stays out.
  • in · Adult LR-MDS (IPSS Low / Int-1), transfusion-dependent (≥4 RBC units / 8 weeks)
  • in · ESA-ineligible or ESA-refractory
  • in · No isolated del(5q) — lenalidomide is the preferred standard there, not RYTELO
  • in · RS-negative, or failed / ineligible for luspatercept — the highest-confidence subpopulation
  • in · The full HCP chain: hematologists, transfusion-medicine physicians, hematopathologists, referring GPs

Where we stop — The Matrix ~8 min

Nine rows. Four columns. Thirty-six cells — and every one of them is an argument you can see and challenge.

This is the moment worth sitting with. Rows and columns are just two of the six dimensions your team kept — picked because they're the ones you'd actually make a resourcing decision on. Swap in a different pair and you're looking at a different strategic question, in under a minute, not a re-commissioned study.

A note on what's illustrative here: every number on this map — patient counts, prices, uptake — and any apparent order among cells (a "ceiling case," a "long tail") are for illustration only, not a validated forecast or a real prioritization. Deciding which segments actually deserve resources takes a proper attractiveness-and-feasibility assessment of each one — market size and growth aren't enough on their own; how winnable each segment really is for RYTELO matters just as much. A proper market access assessment isn't reflected here either: reimbursement status, HTA outcomes, and formulary and funding pathways vary by country and by institution, and they can move a segment's real accessibility more than any of the figures above.
